# Content Style Bible

This document is the source of truth for player-facing tone, thematic framing, and writing guidance across Verbum content.

Use it when creating:
- in-game books,
- profile manuals,
- lore-adjacent text,
- feature names and item flavor,
- player-facing copy that should feel native to Verbum.

This is not a lore encyclopedia.
It is a practical style guide for keeping content coherent.

## Core Spine
Verbum should feel like a world ordered around Christ as center, but by orientation rather than propaganda.

The project should not read like:
- a sermon pasted onto a sandbox,
- generic fantasy with church paint,
- constant quotation or religious signage,
- manipulative or coercive spirituality.

It should read like:
- a world with a hidden geometry of grace,
- architecture, rhythm, return, and care,
- quiet sacredness rather than loud branding,
- human, earthy, worn, beautiful, and intelligible design.

Short creative constitution:

> Systems become more coherent when the player returns, attends, and gives.

## Tone Principles
### What Verbum should feel like
- Quiet, intentional, devotional, and humane.
- Rooted in craftsmanship, stewardship, continuity, and memory.
- Architected rather than improvised.
- Suggestive rather than over-explained.
- Willing to be beautiful without becoming ornamental mush.

### What Verbum should avoid
- Preachiness.
- Constant explicit scripture quotation.
- Generic fantasy mysticism with Christian terms pasted on.
- Edgy grimdark for its own sake.
- Saccharine softness with no texture or gravity.
- Mechanical systems that turn prayer, charity, or devotion into a buff vending machine.

## Sacredness Rules
Sacred language should usually behave like architecture:
- threshold text,
- inscriptions,
- field manuals,
- brief invocations,
- headings,
- marginalia,
- names that imply order and memory.

Do not flood the player with explicit doctrinal exposition unless the content specifically calls for it.
The world should whisper more often than it shouts.

## Two Spiritual Weathers
Verbum draws from two tonal families:

### Cathedral Realism
- Grounded, historical, agricultural, monastic, and human.
- Stone, timber, dust, candlelight, worn cloth, hand-copied text, bells, fields, paths.
- Best for baseline, domestic, pastoral, and practical content.

### Apocalyptic Mythic
- Luminous, symbolic, visionary, uncanny, cosmic, and charged with signs.
- Stars, thresholds, veils, pilgrimage, revelation, strange order, dreamlike pattern.
- Best for expansive exploration, magic, quests, and larger-than-life systems.

These can coexist, but one should dominate depending on the profile.

## Profile Identity
### Veritas
Internal module/build id: `veritas`
Player-facing name: `Veritas`

Purpose:
- the refined baseline,
- foundational, restrained, trustworthy, and clear,
- the simplest expression of Verbum's worldview.

Tone:
- plainspoken,
- grounded,
- modest,
- durable,
- clean and dependable.

Content shape:
- orientation texts,
- foundational manuals,
- essential practices,
- low-drama, high-clarity baseline systems.

If writing a Veritas book, ask:
- does this help the player understand the world calmly?
- does it feel foundational rather than ornamental?

### Vocations
Internal module/build id: `votum`
Current code/build id remains `votum` until a deliberate rename lands.
Player-facing name: `Vocations`

Purpose:
- the calm, pastoral, slow-life expansion above Veritas,
- work, craft, farming, building, household rhythm, stewardship, and daily life.

Tone:
- warm,
- local,
- seasonal,
- domestic,
- artisanal,
- restful without becoming empty.

Content shape:
- farming and garden manuals,
- house and workshop guidance,
- books about rhythms of care, labor, and place,
- texts that honor ordinary life and practical beauty.

What not to do:
- do not make Vocations read like high fantasy,
- do not make it combat-driven,
- do not make it quest-heavy or progression-obsessed,
- do not make it overtly monastic in a way that loses the everyday work theme.

### Visions
Internal module/build id: `visions`
Player-facing name: `Visions`

Purpose:
- the broad, expansive, feature-rich flagship line,
- the place for tech, magic, quests, progression systems, exploration, and wonder.

Tone:
- luminous,
- adventurous,
- learned,
- mythic,
- expansive,
- full of discovery and layered meaning.

Content shape:
- field guides,
- chronicles,
- atlases,
- technical and mystical compendia,
- player-facing systems manuals with symbolic resonance.

Reference frame:
- this is the "large complete modpack" line,
- breadth matters here,
- the world should feel rich, deep, and slightly numinous.

### Vorago
Internal module/build id: `vorago`
Player-facing name: `Vorago`

Purpose:
- the punitive pressure layer above Visions,
- not mainly a second huge content explosion,
- mostly harsher laws, greater friction, and hostile-survival pressure.

Tone:
- severe,
- weathered,
- austere,
- survivalist,
- disciplined,
- unforgiving.

Content shape:
- harsh manuals,
- doctrines of endurance,
- guidance for scarcity, exposure, damage, hunger, thirst, cold, attrition, and risk.

Reference frame:
- more RLCraft-like pressure,
- more demanding survival constraints,
- less "new wonder" than Visions,
- more "the same world, but it bites harder."

What not to do:
- do not turn it into juvenile grimdark,
- do not make it cartoonishly evil,
- do not make it nihilistic.

Vorago should feel like an abyss faced with discipline, not chaos for chaos's sake.

## Writing In-Game Books
### Primary role
Library books should usually function as:
- profile manuals,
- field texts,
- orientation aids,
- reflective guides,
- practical references.

They can carry lore and theology, but they should still help the player.

### Default structure
For most guide/manual books, prefer this order:
1. What this book is.
2. How this profile or system feels.
3. What the player should pay attention to.
4. Practical guidance.
5. A short closing note or invocation.

### Page voice
Pages should usually be:
- short,
- legible,
- calm,
- varied in rhythm,
- more inscriptional than chatty.

Avoid:
- giant dense walls of prose,
- meme humor,
- modern snark,
- wiki-style over-explanation on every page.

### Book categories by profile
- Veritas: devotional primer, handbook, orientation text.
- Vocations: almanac, household guide, craft notebook, seasonal manual.
- Visions: atlas, chronicle, compendium, field guide.
- Vorago: rule, survival doctrine, ash-book, severe handbook.

## Naming Guidance
Names should feel:
- memorable,
- dignified,
- slightly symbolic,
- usable as real item names.

Good patterns:
- `Dusty Devotional`
- `Book of Hours`
- `Pilgrim's Atlas`
- `Rule of Ashes`

Avoid:
- joke names,
- faux-Latin clutter,
- generic fantasy nouns with no lived texture,
- overly explicit doctrinal names unless the content truly warrants it.

## Explicitness Guidance
Use direct Christian language when it serves the content.
Do not force it into every surface.

Good uses:
- a devotional,
- a chapel inscription,
- a prayer book,
- a feast or liturgical reference,
- a manuscript-like heading.

Use restraint when:
- writing generalized system guides,
- naming practical tools,
- building broad exploration content that should remain discoverable and hospitable.

## Manuals vs Lore
When in doubt, choose manuals over lore.

The first wave of library content should help the player:
- understand a profile,
- understand a feature family,
- enter the world with the right posture.

Lore should support that, not replace it.

## Agent Rule Of Thumb
Before writing player-facing content, ask:
1. Which profile owns this content?
2. Is this more manual, inscription, chronicle, or doctrine?
3. Does the wording feel quiet and intentional?
4. Would this still read well to a player who does not care about theology?
5. Does it help the player, not just decorate the repo?

If the answer to 4 is no, soften it.
If the answer to 5 is no, rewrite it.
